# Writing Guide

The default message is a job-application follow-up, not a sales sequence. It borrows the useful parts of short cold-email research while removing claims that do not fit recruiting.

## Sources Used

Instantly's public guidance repeatedly favors short copy, a specific opening, one easy ask, and clear subjects. Its examples place high-performing cold emails below 80 words and recommend using a real detail about the recipient or context rather than a generic introduction.

- [How to improve cold email reply rates](https://instantly.ai/blog/how-to-improve-your-cold-email-reply-rate/)
- [Cold email guide](https://www.instantly.ai/blog/cold-email/)
- [Cold email sequence practices](https://instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-sequence-best-practices/)
- [Cold and warm subject lines](https://instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-subject-lines-vs-warm-outreach-whats-different-why-it-matters/)

The supplied video transcript used the same basic rule: fewer than 80 words, four sentences, and one call to action.

## Job-Search Adjustment

A sales email often claims a problem and offers an outcome. A job applicant usually cannot know the company's internal problem, so that structure would encourage invented pain points and inflated claims.

The console uses this version instead:

1. Mention one factual responsibility from the listing.
2. Connect one supplied point of experience to that work.
3. State that the application has already been submitted.
4. Ask one routing question.

This keeps the useful specificity and brevity without pretending to know more than the posting provides.

## Default Guardrails

- Count the greeting and sign-off inside the 80-word limit.
- Use one question and one call to action.
- Keep the subject short and clear; include the position ID when useful.
- Preserve the correct job hyperlink and resume.
- Do not start with "I recently applied."
- Do not invent praise, company problems, experience, metrics, names, or IDs.
- Do not use urgency, sales claims, or generic compliments.
- Keep every recipient's draft editable and separately reviewable.

Follow-up timing remains a workflow setting. Sales-sequence advice should not automatically become recruiter cadence. Company history, replies, cooldowns, and the operator's instructions take priority.
